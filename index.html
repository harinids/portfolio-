§ 01 — Specimen Record

About

A model remembers what the database forgot. Erase a record from storage and the model still acts on it, because what it learned from that record lives in its weights. That gap is the question I work on: how do you make a model forget specifically what it shouldn't have learned, and how do you check that it did?

My first project, DAMU, asked what happens when the data itself shifts. It detects drift in a hate-speech stream and deactivates only the affected parts of the model, without retraining. It caught every drift event with no false alarms on a 38K-sample benchmark, and it is under review at IEEE TETCI. I then took the idea to federated settings, where drift-segmented checkpointing lets a system erase one time window while keeping what it learned afterward. In LLMs, I'm studying continual unlearning with LoRA and EWC. There, a model reported successful forgetting while its perplexity had quietly risen from 16 to over 1,300, a failure that standard checks missed.

That experience shaped how I work. I treat evaluation as part of the research: I switched my membership-inference test from loss to confidence because loss could be gamed, and I report results across seeds rather than one lucky run.

I also build systems people use. Nurture turns blood pressure, haemoglobin and sugar readings into a single triage flag for ASHA health workers, and at Deepspatial I built the ontology behind a GeoAI system that matches farm-level gaps to government schemes. I'm drawn to problems where a model has to adapt without losing what it already knows.

IEEE Transactions on Computational Social Systems · 2026
DAMU: Drift-Aware Machine Unlearning for Adaptive Hate Speech Detection Under Temporal Distribution Shift

Problem: Hate-speech classifiers degrade as language and platform norms drift over time; full retraining is required to unlearn stale patterns, making continuous compliance prohibitively expensive.

Method: A four-phase pipeline coupling ADWIN drift signals directly to SISA shard deactivation — automating forget-set construction and removing the need for manual specification. Drift detection → forget-set construction → SISA unlearning → ensemble aggregation.

Drift TPR 1.00 / FPR 0.00 Forget-set accuracy −13.4 pp Retain-set accuracy +6.5 pp Forget-set loss +0.2207 Retain-set loss −0.0953 38,000-sample dataset (Davidson + OLID)

Manuscript in preparation · 2026
FDAM-UL: Federated Drift-Aware Machine Unlearning

Problem: Erasing specific data from a federated model is hard when client data distributions drift over time — existing methods cannot separate pre-drift from post-drift knowledge without exposing client data.

Method: A federated framework (FedProx) combining ADWIN + KS-test drift detection, drift-segmented checkpointing, and EWC-regularized gradient ascent. No raw client data leaves the device.

Forgetting score 1.000 (5 seeds, 2 datasets) CICIDS2017 + UNSW-NB15 GDPR Art. 17-motivated FedProx aggregation
In Preparation
Manuscript in preparation · 2026
Continual Unlearning with Memory Consolidation in Large Language Models

Problem: LLMs need to selectively forget harmful or private knowledge without catastrophic forgetting of essential capabilities — and without full retraining.

Method: EWC-based selective unlearning + memory consolidation on GPT-2 with LoRA; evaluated on TOFU and HateXplain benchmarks across retain/forget/eval splits. Built two novel components: Knowledge Auditor and Stability–Plasticity Controller, balancing new learning against knowledge retention.

Research Intern — Machine Learning & AI Systems
Techno International New Town · Kolkata
  • Developed DAMU, a pipeline that couples ADWIN drift detection with SISA unlearning so the drift signal automatically becomes the forget set, with no manual specification or full retraining.
  • Built the pipeline in Python and PyTorch, with shards aligned to ten temporal windows so drifted data can be removed by deactivating whole shards.
  • Evaluated on a 38,000-sample hate speech corpus (Davidson and OLID) with harmful content shifting from 40% to 85%. Achieved 100% drift detection with zero false positives.
  • Passed all four unlearning verification criteria: forget-set accuracy down 13.4 points (80.1% to 66.7%), retain-set accuracy up 6.5 points (69.7% to 76.2%), forget loss up from 0.416 to 0.637, retain loss down from 0.600 to 0.505.
  • Unlearning cost was zero retraining, since 5 of 10 shards were deactivated outright. Manuscript under review at IEEE TETCI.
